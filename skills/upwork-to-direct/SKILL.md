# Upwork to Direct — Transition Strategy

## Why Go Direct?

### Fee Comparison
| Arrangement | On $5,000 payment |
|-------------|:-----------------:|
| Upwork (first $500K with client) | $500 (10%) |
| Upwork (after $10K with same client) | $250 (5%) |
| Direct via Cenoa | ~$40 (<1%) |
| Direct via Wise | ~$75 (1.5%) |
| Direct via PayPal | ~$200 (4%) |

**Annual savings on a $3K/month retainer:**
- Upwork (10%): $3,600/year in fees
- Direct via Cenoa: ~$430/year in fees
- **Savings: $3,170/year**

## When to Transition

### Timing Checklist
- [ ] Completed 2+ successful projects with this client on Upwork
- [ ] Received positive feedback and built mutual trust
- [ ] Client has expressed satisfaction and interest in ongoing work
- [ ] You have the client's direct contact info (exchanged naturally during the project)
- [ ] The relationship feels "ready" — more like a partnership than a marketplace transaction

### Wait Until You Have
- Strong JSS that won't be hurt by one fewer active contract
- Enough other Upwork clients that this isn't your only income source
- A signed direct contract ready to go

## The Pitch — How to Frame It

### Approach 1: The "Better for Both of Us" Frame
> "I've really enjoyed working together on Upwork. As we move into ongoing work, I wanted to suggest we move to a direct arrangement. Here's why it benefits both of us:
>
> - **For you:** Lower costs — Upwork adds 3-5% to what you pay me, which you wouldn't pay directly.
> - **For me:** I can offer you a slightly better rate since I'm not losing 10-20% to the platform.
> - **For both:** Faster payments, simpler communication, no platform restrictions.
>
> I'd set up proper invoicing and we'd sign a simple service agreement. Happy to discuss if you're interested."

### Approach 2: The Natural Evolution
> "Now that we've built a solid working relationship, it makes sense to simplify things. Many of my long-term clients prefer direct contracts — it's more flexible, faster, and more cost-effective for both sides. Want me to put together a proposal for a direct retainer?"

### Communication
- Move to email for formal communication
- Slack/WhatsApp for day-to-day (whatever the client prefers)
- Video calls via Google Meet/Zoom (no platform restrictions)

## Risk Mitigation

### Protecting Yourself
- **Always have a signed contract** before starting direct work
- **Always get a deposit** (50% minimum for new direct arrangements)
- **Keep records:** Invoices, contracts, communication logs
- **Start small:** First direct project should be modest. Build trust off-platform gradually.

### Upwork TOS Considerations
- Upwork's Terms of Service prohibit circumventing their fee structure for clients you found through the platform
- The restriction has a 2-year window from last contract
- In practice, once you have a genuine long-term relationship and the client wants to go direct, the practical risk is low
- **Best practice:** Complete your Upwork contracts properly, leave positive reviews for each other, and transition to direct naturally
- **Never:** Ask a client to go off-platform on the first contract, or discuss going direct in Upwork messages (they're monitored)
