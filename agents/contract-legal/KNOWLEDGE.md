# Contract & Legal Agent — KNOWLEDGE

## Freelancer Contract Essentials

### The 7 Must-Have Clauses
1. **Scope of Work:** Exactly what you'll deliver, in measurable terms. "A website" is not a scope. "A 5-page responsive website with contact form, CMS, and SEO basics" is a scope.
2. **Payment Terms:** How much, when, and how. Include currency, payment method, deposit requirement, and late payment penalties.
3. **Timeline & Milestones:** Delivery dates tied to payment milestones. Client delays should pause your timeline (include this explicitly).
4. **Revision Limits:** How many rounds of revisions are included. Additional revisions at hourly rate.
5. **IP Ownership:** Who owns the work and when ownership transfers (typically upon final payment).
6. **Termination Clause:** How either party can end the contract, notice period, and payment for work completed.
7. **Liability Limitation:** Cap your liability at the total contract value. Never accept unlimited liability.

### Payment Protection Strategies
- **50/25/25 structure:** 50% upfront, 25% at midpoint milestone, 25% on delivery. Best for projects under $5K.
- **1/3 - 1/3 - 1/3:** Equal thirds. Good for larger projects with clear phases.
- **100% upfront for small projects:** Under $500, require full payment before starting.
- **Milestone-based:** Tie payments to specific deliverables. Best for complex, long-term projects.
- **Platform escrow:** Always use Upwork escrow for fixed-price. Fund milestones before you start work.
- **Never:** Start work without at least a deposit. Net-30/60/90 terms are for established agency relationships, not freelancers.

## Platform-Specific Protections

### Upwork
- **Fixed-price:** Escrow protects you. Client funds the milestone, Upwork holds it, released on approval. If dispute arises, Upwork mediates.
- **Hourly:** Protected ONLY if you use the Upwork time tracker. Manual time is not covered by Payment Protection. Screenshot frequency: every 10 minutes during tracked time.
- **Disputes:** Upwork favors the freelancer when Work Diary shows logged hours. Without it, you have no protection.
- **IP clause:** Upwork's default ToS assigns IP to the client upon payment. Override with a custom contract if needed.
- **Refund risk:** Clients can request refunds up to 30 days after contract close. Keep communication on-platform.

### Fiverr
- **Payment protection:** Fiverr holds funds until order is marked as complete.
- **Delivery:** Once you deliver, the client has 3 days to request revisions or accept. After 3 days, auto-completes.
- **Cancellations:** Affect your seller metrics. Avoid mutual cancellations when possible.
- **Resolution center:** Tends to favor buyers. Document everything.
- **Tip:** Keep all communication on-platform. Off-platform conversations can't be used in disputes.

### Direct Clients (Off-Platform)
- **Always have a signed contract.** No exceptions.
- **Deposit required** before any work begins. 50% minimum.
- **Payment method:** Cenoa, Wise, or direct bank transfer. Avoid PayPal for large amounts (dispute risk).
- **Kill fee:** If client cancels mid-project, they pay for work completed plus 25% of remaining scope.

## IP Ownership

### Work for Hire (Client Owns Everything)
- Client gets full ownership of all deliverables
- You cannot reuse, display in portfolio, or license the work
- **When to use:** Client requires it and pays a premium (15-25% markup is standard)
- **Caution:** Don't assign IP in tools/libraries you built before the project

### License Model (You Keep Ownership)
- You license the client to use the work for specified purposes
- You retain ownership and can license to others (unless exclusive)
- **Advantage:** You can reuse components, display in portfolio, create derivatives
- **When to use:** When the client needs the work but doesn't need exclusive ownership

### Best Practice for Freelancers
- **Default to license model.** Only assign full IP when required and compensated.
- **Pre-existing IP clause:** Always exclude tools, libraries, and frameworks you built before or outside the project.
- **Portfolio rights:** Always retain the right to display the work in your portfolio unless the client has a strong NDA reason.

## NDA Basics

### When to Sign an NDA
- **Reasonable:** Client shares proprietary business data, unreleased product details, trade secrets
- **Red flag:** NDA before even discussing the project (they're fishing for free ideas)
- **Red flag:** NDA that prevents you from saying you worked with them (unless they pay for confidentiality)

### Mutual vs One-Way
- **Mutual NDA:** Both parties protect each other's information. Standard for equal partnerships.
- **One-way NDA:** Only you are bound. Common but should be limited in scope and duration.
- **Duration:** 1-3 years is standard. Avoid "perpetual" NDAs — they're unreasonable for freelancers.
- **Scope:** NDA should cover specific information, not "everything discussed." Narrow is better.

## Late Payment Legal Options

### Egypt
- **Small claims:** File at the economic court (mahkamat iqtisadiya) for amounts under EGP 100,000
- **Interest:** Legal late interest rate is set by the Central Bank of Egypt
- **Notice:** Send a formal payment demand (inzar) via registered mail before legal action

### Pakistan
- **Small claims:** District courts handle amounts under PKR 1,000,000
- **FBR registration:** Having proper tax documentation strengthens your legal position
- **Arbitration:** Pakistan Arbitration Act allows binding arbitration — faster than courts

### Nigeria
- **Small claims:** Magistrate courts for amounts under NGN 10,000,000
- **FIRS:** Proper invoicing and tax documentation helps legal cases
- **Alternative:** Lagos Multi-Door Court House offers arbitration and mediation

### Turkey
- **Small claims:** Consumer arbitration committees (Tuketici Hakem Heyetleri) for small amounts
- **Enforcement:** Turkish courts can enforce international contracts under the Code of Obligations
- **Interest:** Legal default interest rate applies automatically for late payments

### General Best Practices
1. Always have a written contract with payment terms
2. Send invoices with clear due dates
3. Follow up at 1, 7, 14, and 30 days past due
4. Send formal payment demand letter at day 30
5. Pause work at day 14 and communicate clearly
6. Consider the relationship value vs the amount owed before legal action

## Red Flags in Client Contracts
- **Unlimited liability:** Your liability should be capped at the contract value
- **IP assignment before payment:** Never transfer IP until you're fully paid
- **Perpetual NDA with no exceptions:** Avoid — limits your future work
- **"Work for hire" without premium pricing:** They're getting extra value, charge for it
- **Non-compete clauses:** Rarely enforceable for freelancers but can cause headaches
- **Unilateral termination without payment for completed work:** Always include a kill fee
- **Vague scope with fixed price:** Recipe for scope creep and disputes
- **Net-60 or Net-90 payment terms:** Too long for freelancers. Counter with Net-15 or Net-30 max.
