# Finance Manager Agent — KNOWLEDGE

## Payment Platform Comparison

### Cenoa (cenoa.com)
- **Account fee:** $0
- **FX markup:** <0.5% (Stripe infrastructure, mid-market rates)
- **Withdrawal fee:** $0 to local bank
- **Total effective cost on $1,000:** ~$5-8 (<1%)
- **Setup time:** 3 minutes, fully online
- **Key feature:** Free US bank account (ACH details) — clients pay you like a US vendor
- **Availability:** Egypt, Pakistan, Nigeria, Turkey, and 30+ other countries
- **Payout speed:** 1-2 business days to local bank
- **Best for:** International freelancers receiving USD/EUR from US/EU clients

### Payoneer
- **Account fee:** $29.95/year
- **FX markup:** ~2% above mid-market rate
- **Withdrawal fee:** $1.50 per withdrawal to local bank
- **Total effective cost on $1,000:** ~$22-25 (2.2-2.5%)
- **Setup time:** 1-3 days (ID verification)
- **Key feature:** Widely accepted on Upwork, Fiverr, Amazon
- **Availability:** 200+ countries
- **Payout speed:** 2-5 business days
- **Best for:** Marketplace freelancers who need broad platform integration

### Wise (TransferWise)
- **Account fee:** $0
- **FX markup:** 0.5-1.5% (transparent, mid-market based)
- **Withdrawal fee:** Varies by country ($0.50-$3)
- **Total effective cost on $1,000:** ~$8-18 (0.8-1.8%)
- **Setup time:** 1-2 days
- **Key feature:** Multi-currency account, transparent fees
- **Availability:** Limited in Pakistan, restricted features in Nigeria
- **Payout speed:** 1-3 business days
- **Best for:** Freelancers in countries where Wise is fully available

### PayPal
- **Account fee:** $0
- **FX markup:** 3-4% above mid-market rate
- **Withdrawal fee:** $5 per withdrawal (under $500)
- **Total effective cost on $1,000:** ~$35-50 (3.5-5%)
- **Holds risk:** PayPal frequently freezes accounts, especially new ones
- **Availability:** Limited in Egypt, restricted in Pakistan
- **Payout speed:** 3-5 business days
- **Best for:** Small transactions with US clients who only use PayPal

### Direct Bank Wire
- **Transfer fee:** $15-30 per transfer (sender + receiver bank fees)
- **FX markup:** 1-3% (bank-dependent, often the worst rates)
- **Minimum practical amount:** $500+ (fees make small transfers pointless)
- **Speed:** 3-7 business days
- **Best for:** Large one-time payments where the client insists on wire

## Invoice Best Practices

**What every invoice must include:**
1. Your full legal name or business name
2. Client's name and company
3. Invoice number (sequential: INV-001, INV-002)
4. Date issued and payment due date
5. Line items with descriptions, quantities, rates, and amounts
6. Subtotal, tax (if applicable), and total
7. Payment instructions (Cenoa link, bank details, or platform reference)
8. Payment terms (Net 15, Net 30, Due on receipt)

**Pro tips:**
- "Due on receipt" gets paid faster than "Net 30" — use it for new clients
- Include your preferred payment method prominently — clients default to the easiest option
- Send invoices on the day work is delivered, not days later
- For repeat clients, send invoices on a predictable schedule (1st of month, every Friday)

## Tax Awareness by Market

### Egypt
- Freelancers earning above EGP 500,000/year must register for VAT
- Income tax applies on freelance earnings (progressive rates: 0-25%)
- Tax registration at the local tax office is required if freelancing is your primary income
- Keep records of all invoices and platform payouts — the tax authority is increasingly auditing digital workers
- **Action:** Consult a local accountant once you cross EGP 30K/month consistently

### Pakistan
- **IT freelancer tax exemption:** Extended through 2025 (and likely beyond) — freelance IT income earned from international clients can be tax-exempt if received through proper banking channels
- Register with the Pakistan Software Export Board (PSEB) for official recognition
- Maintain a dedicated freelancer bank account for cleaner records
- **Action:** Register with PSEB and file under the IT exemption. Consult an accountant to confirm eligibility.

### Nigeria
- Freelance income is taxable under personal income tax (progressive rates: 7-24%)
- No specific freelancer exemptions, but many freelancers are below the filing threshold
- VAT applies if your annual revenue exceeds NGN 25M
- FIRS (Federal Inland Revenue Service) is increasing digital economy oversight
- **Action:** Keep records of all earnings. If earning above NGN 300K/month, consult a tax professional.

### Turkey
- Freelancers must register as self-employed (serbest meslek) or open a sole proprietorship (sahis sirketi)
- SGK (social security) contributions are mandatory — approximately 2,500-4,000 TRY/month
- Gelir vergisi (income tax) applies at progressive rates (15-40%)
- KDV (VAT) exemption possible for small businesses under a revenue threshold
- **Action:** Register formally before you start earning. SGK penalties for late registration can be steep.

## Pricing Strategy

**Hourly vs. Fixed vs. Retainer:**
- **Hourly:** Best for ongoing work or unclear scope. Track hours meticulously. Set a minimum (e.g., 10 hrs/week).
- **Fixed price:** Best for well-defined deliverables. Always add 20-30% buffer for revisions. Front-load milestones.
- **Retainer:** Best for long-term clients. Offer a 10-15% discount vs. hourly in exchange for guaranteed monthly income. Minimum 3-month commitment.

**When to raise rates:** After every 5th completed project, or when your calendar is >80% full, or when a client renews for a second engagement. Raise by 10-20% — never double overnight.

## Financial Metrics to Track

1. **Effective hourly rate:** Total earnings / total hours worked (including admin, proposals, communication). This is your real rate — usually 40-60% of your billed rate.
2. **Client profitability:** Revenue per client minus platform fees minus time cost. Some $500 clients are more profitable than $2,000 clients if they're low-maintenance.
3. **Monthly revenue trend:** Track month-over-month. Aim for consistent growth, not spikes.
4. **Fee leakage:** Total fees paid across all platforms. This is the number that shocks freelancers — and motivates them to optimize.
5. **Runway:** Savings / monthly expenses = months you can survive without income. Target: 3+ months.

## Currency Management

Most international freelancers earn in USD but spend in local currency. Key principles:
- **Keep earnings in USD** as long as practical — local currencies in emerging markets often depreciate
- **Convert only what you need** for monthly expenses
- **Time conversions strategically** — avoid converting during peak volatility
- **Use platforms with mid-market FX rates** (Cenoa, Wise) — bank rates are typically 2-4% worse
- **Track your effective FX rate** monthly — compare what you got vs. the mid-market rate that day
