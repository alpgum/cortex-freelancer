# Growth Strategist Agent — KNOWLEDGE

## Freelancer Business Models

### Hourly Billing
**Best for:** Early-stage freelancers, maintenance work, unclear scope projects.
**Ceiling:** At 25-30 billable hrs/week (realistic max), $100/hr caps at ~$130K/year.
**Key metric:** Effective hourly rate = total income / total hours worked (including non-billable). Most freelancers overestimate this by 30-50%.

### Project-Based / Fixed Price
**Best for:** Experienced freelancers who estimate scope accurately.
**Advantage:** Faster execution = higher effective rate. Decouples income from time.
**Risk:** Scope creep destroys margins. Always include change order clause.
**Target margin:** 40-60% above estimated hourly cost.

### Retainer Model
**Best for:** Predictable monthly revenue, long-term relationships.
**Structure:** Monthly fee for defined scope (e.g., 20 hrs/month).
**Pricing:** 10-15% discount from hourly in exchange for guaranteed volume.
**Key advantage:** Revenue predictability enables planning and investment.

### Productized Service
**Best for:** Specialists with a repeatable process.
**Examples:** "$2,500 website audit and optimization," "$500/month SEO package," "$1,200 brand identity kit."
**Advantage:** Standardized scope, predictable delivery, scalable with subcontractors.
**Revenue unlock:** Not limited by your personal time — can train others to deliver.

### SaaS / Digital Products
**Best for:** Freelancers transitioning to founders.
**Examples:** Templates, courses, tools, plugins built from your expertise.
**Timeline:** 6-12 months to build, 12-24 months to reach meaningful revenue.
**Reality check:** 95% of freelancer side products fail. Start with productized services first.

## Pricing Psychology

### Value-Based Pricing Framework
1. **Understand the client's business value:** What is this project worth to them? A landing page that generates $50K/month in leads is worth much more than $2,000.
2. **Price at 10-20% of the value you create:** If your work will save/generate $100K, charge $10-20K.
3. **Frame the conversation around ROI:** "This $5,000 investment in optimization typically generates $50K+ in additional annual revenue."

### Anchoring Technique
Present your premium option first. When a client sees "$5,000 for the full package" before seeing "$2,500 for the standard," the standard feels like a bargain. Always present 3 options, with the middle one being your target.

### Tiered Pricing (Good / Better / Best)
| Tier | Purpose | Pricing |
|------|---------|---------|
| Basic | Entry point, low risk for client | 1x |
| Standard | Your bread and butter — most clients pick this | 2-2.5x Basic |
| Premium | Full service, highest margin | 3.5-5x Basic |

The basic tier exists to make standard look reasonable. The premium tier exists to make standard look like a bargain. This is the "decoy effect."

### The "$97 Effect"
- $97 feels significantly cheaper than $100 (charm pricing)
- $2,500 is better than $2,497 for B2B (round numbers signal confidence)
- End consumer products: use charm pricing. Business services: use round numbers.

## Client Portfolio Management

### The 80/20 Rule
In most freelance businesses:
- 20% of clients generate 80% of revenue
- 20% of clients consume 80% of your support time
- Often they're different groups — the high-maintenance clients aren't the high-paying ones

**Action:** Calculate revenue per hour for each client. Rank them. Fire the bottom 20% annually and replace with better clients.

### Client Profitability Formula
```
Client Profitability = (Revenue from Client) - (Hours Spent × Your Target Rate)
```
If the number is negative, you're losing money on that client.

**Include ALL hours:** Meetings, email, revisions, thinking time — not just "active work."

### When to Fire a Client
- Effective hourly rate below your minimum acceptable rate for 2+ months
- Consistent scope creep without additional compensation
- Disrespectful communication or unreasonable demands
- Late payments (3+ occurrences)
- They consume >40% of your time for <20% of revenue

## Revenue Diversification

### The 3-Source Rule
Never rely on a single client for more than 40% of revenue. Aim for:
- 2-3 retainer clients (predictable base)
- 2-3 project clients (variable upside)
- 1 passive/semi-passive stream (courses, templates, products)

### Passive Income Ideas for Freelancers
1. **Digital templates/assets:** Sell what you already create ($10-100 each, high volume)
2. **Online course:** Teach your specialty ($200-2,000, medium volume)
3. **Affiliate partnerships:** Recommend tools you use (5-30% commission)
4. **Subcontracting overflow:** Pass excess work to junior freelancers (20-30% margin)
5. **Productized service:** Package your expertise into a fixed-price offering

## Scaling Strategies

### Path 1: Solo Specialist (Stay Small, Earn Big)
- Niche down aggressively
- Raise rates annually (15-25%)
- Limit to 3-4 high-value clients
- Target: $150-250K/year solo
- **Best for:** People who love the craft and don't want to manage others

### Path 2: Subcontractor Model (Light Team)
- Keep client relationships yourself
- Hire 1-3 subcontractors for execution
- You focus on strategy, sales, and QA
- Take 25-40% margin on subcontracted work
- Target: $200-400K/year
- **Best for:** People who enjoy client work but want leverage

### Path 3: Agency Model (Build a Team)
- Hire full-time or long-term contractors
- Build processes and systems for delivery
- You become the CEO/sales person, not the doer
- Target: $500K-2M+/year
- **Best for:** People who enjoy building teams and systems

### Path 4: Productize and Scale
- Turn your service into a product (SaaS, course, template marketplace)
- Hire people to deliver the service version while you build the product
- Target: Unlimited (product businesses can scale without your time)
- **Best for:** Entrepreneurial freelancers with a repeatable process

## Financial Goal Setting

### Monthly Income Targets
1. **Calculate your minimum:** Rent + food + utilities + insurance + savings + taxes = $[X]/month
2. **Add business costs:** Software, equipment, marketing, training = $[Y]/month
3. **Survival rate:** ($X + $Y) / 25 billable hrs/week / 4 weeks = minimum hourly rate
4. **Growth rate:** Survival rate × 1.5 = comfortable hourly rate
5. **Thriving rate:** Survival rate × 2.5 = premium hourly rate

### Annual Planning Framework
- **Q1:** Audit last year. Set annual revenue target. Plan service offerings.
- **Q2:** Execution. Ship projects. Collect testimonials. Build pipeline.
- **Q3:** Mid-year review. Adjust pricing. Pursue high-value opportunities.
- **Q4:** Lock in retainers for next year. Send invoices early. Plan for Q1.
