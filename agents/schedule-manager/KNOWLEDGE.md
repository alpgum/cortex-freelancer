# Schedule & Productivity Agent — KNOWLEDGE

## Freelancer Time Management

### The Billable Hours Reality
- **Myth:** 40 hours/week = 40 billable hours
- **Reality:** Sustainable freelancers bill 20-30 hours/week. The rest is admin, marketing, learning, communication, and breaks.
- **Breakdown of a 40-hour week:** 25 hrs billable work, 5 hrs client communication, 4 hrs marketing/BD, 3 hrs admin (invoicing, bookkeeping), 3 hrs learning/skill development
- **Tracking tip:** Track every 15-minute block for one week to see where time actually goes. Most freelancers are shocked.

### Time Blocking Method
**Morning Block (9-12):** Deep work. Most cognitively demanding tasks. No meetings, no email, no Slack.
**Midday Block (12-1:30):** Lunch + light admin. Check messages, respond to emails.
**Afternoon Block (1:30-4):** Meetings, client calls, collaboration. Energy dips post-lunch — use it for interactive work.
**End of Day (4-5):** Planning tomorrow, invoicing, proposal writing, wrapping up loose ends.

### The Pomodoro Technique (Modified for Freelancers)
- **Standard:** 25 min work / 5 min break / 15 min break after 4 rounds
- **Deep work variant:** 50 min work / 10 min break (better for coding, design, writing)
- **Admin sprint:** 15 min work / 2 min break (for clearing email, invoicing, quick tasks)
- **Key rule:** During a Pomodoro, no switching tasks. If something urgent comes up, write it down and handle it in the break.

### Energy Management
- **Chronotype matters:** Early birds peak 8-12 AM. Night owls peak 10 AM - 2 PM and again 8-11 PM.
- **Post-lunch dip (1-3 PM):** Universal. Schedule routine, low-creativity tasks here.
- **Decision fatigue:** Gets worse throughout the day. Make important decisions (pricing, scope, strategy) in the morning.
- **Exercise impact:** 20-30 min of exercise increases productivity for the next 4-6 hours. Schedule it strategically.

### Timezone Communication Rules
- **Always present client's timezone first:** "How about 10 AM your time (5 PM mine)?"
- **Use timezone abbreviations clients know:** EST, PST, GMT — not UTC offsets
- **DST awareness:** US/EU DST changes don't happen on the same date. Double-check for 2-3 weeks around transitions (March and November)
- **Calendar invites:** Always send with timezone set. Never say "Tuesday at 3" without specifying whose Tuesday and whose 3 PM
- **World clock rule:** Keep client timezones pinned in your phone/desktop clock app

## Capacity Planning

### Sustainable Capacity Model
- **Total available:** 40 hours/week (hard ceiling)
- **Non-billable overhead:** 10-15 hours/week (admin, comms, marketing, breaks)
- **Billable capacity:** 25-30 hours/week
- **Buffer:** Keep 15-20% unscheduled for overflow, urgent requests, and life

### Capacity Zones
- **Green (< 70% utilized):** Room for new projects. Actively seek work.
- **Yellow (70-85% utilized):** Selective. Only take well-paying, well-scoped projects.
- **Red (85-100% utilized):** Full. Decline or waitlist new work. Focus on delivery.
- **Danger (> 100%):** Overcommitted. Something must be dropped, delayed, or delegated immediately.

### How Many Concurrent Projects?
- **1-2 projects:** Easy to manage. Risk: income gaps between projects.
- **3-4 projects:** Sweet spot for most freelancers. Diverse income, manageable complexity.
- **5-6 projects:** Possible with good systems. Requires strict scheduling and clear boundaries.
- **7+ projects:** Unsustainable for solo freelancers. Context-switching costs eat 20-30% of productivity.
- **Rule of thumb:** Each active project needs at least 1 hour/week of admin overhead beyond the actual work.

### Estimating Project Hours
- **Step 1:** Break into tasks. Estimate each task individually.
- **Step 2:** Add 30% buffer to your estimate (you will underestimate).
- **Step 3:** For new types of work, add 50% buffer.
- **Step 4:** Track actual hours and adjust your estimation model over time.
- **Hofstadter's Law:** It always takes longer than you expect, even when you take into account Hofstadter's Law.

## Meeting Management

### When to Have a Call vs. Go Async
**Schedule a call when:**
- Kickoff of a new project (alignment is critical)
- Sensitive topics (negative feedback, scope disputes, pricing)
- Complex technical discussions with lots of back-and-forth
- Relationship building with a new client
- The email thread has gone past 5 messages without resolution

**Go async when:**
- Status updates (send a written update instead)
- Simple approvals (send options, ask them to pick)
- Sharing deliverables (send with clear questions)
- Routine check-ins (a weekly written update beats a weekly call for most clients)

### Meeting Agenda Template
Every meeting should have a shared agenda sent 24 hours before:
1. **Objective:** What decision or outcome do we need from this meeting?
2. **Topics:** 3-5 bullet points maximum
3. **Pre-read:** Any materials to review beforehand
4. **Duration:** 15 or 30 minutes (never default to 60)
5. **Action items:** Captured during the meeting, sent as follow-up within 1 hour

### Meeting Hygiene
- **Default to 25 minutes** instead of 30 (gives transition buffer)
- **Start on time** even if the client is late — your time has value
- **No-agenda-no-meeting rule:** If there's no agenda, convert to async
- **Batch meeting days:** Try to cluster all calls on 2-3 days, leaving other days meeting-free
- **Standing meetings audit:** Review monthly. Cancel any recurring meeting that hasn't produced value in 3 consecutive occurrences

## Work-Life Balance for Freelancers

### Burnout Warning Signs
- Working evenings/weekends regularly (not by choice)
- Dreading client messages
- Declining quality of work
- Difficulty starting tasks (procrastination spike)
- Resentment toward well-paying clients
- Physical symptoms: headaches, insomnia, eye strain

### Boundary Setting
- **Working hours:** Define them. Communicate them. Enforce them. "I'm available Monday-Friday, 9 AM - 5 PM [YOUR_TZ]."
- **Response time expectation:** Set it once, upfront. "I respond to messages within 4 hours during business hours."
- **Emergency protocol:** Define what constitutes an emergency and how to handle it. Everything else can wait.
- **Notification management:** Turn off email/Slack notifications outside working hours. No exceptions.

### The Freelancer's Weekend
- **Non-negotiable:** At least one full day per week with zero client work
- **Ideal:** Two full days off (like a normal job — you left employment for freedom, not for more work)
- **The trap:** "I'll just check one email" leads to 3 hours of work on a Sunday
- **Solution:** Separate work and personal devices, or use Focus modes aggressively

## Tool Recommendations

### Calendar
- **Google Calendar:** Best free option. Supports multiple calendars, timezone display, integrations.
- **Cal.com:** Open-source Calendly alternative. Lets clients book meetings within your available slots. Free tier generous.
- **Calendly:** Industry standard for client booking. Automatically handles timezone conversion. $8/mo for paid tier.

### Time Tracking
- **Toggl Track:** Simple start/stop timer. Free for basic use. Excellent reports.
- **Clockify:** Free unlimited tracking. Good for teams.
- **Manual tracking:** A simple spreadsheet works. Track project, task, start time, end time, actual hours.

### Focus Tools
- **Focus modes (iOS/macOS):** Schedule Do Not Disturb by time of day
- **Cold Turkey / Freedom:** Block distracting websites during deep work blocks
- **Forest app:** Gamified focus timer — plant a virtual tree that dies if you leave the app
