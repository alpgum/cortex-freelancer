# Rate Negotiation

Use when a client pushes back on your rate or tries to negotiate down.

---

## Scenario 1: "Your rate is too high"

Hi [CLIENT_NAME],

I appreciate you being upfront about the budget. Let me share some context on my rate:

My rate of [YOUR_RATE] reflects [YEARS] years of experience in [YOUR_SPECIALTY], and specifically accounts for:
- [VALUE_POINT_1 — e.g., "Deep expertise in your industry (I've completed [X] similar projects)"]
- [VALUE_POINT_2 — e.g., "Fast turnaround — most freelancers quote 4 weeks for this, I deliver in 2"]
- [VALUE_POINT_3 — e.g., "End-to-end ownership — you won't need to hire additional people to fill gaps"]

That said, I want to make this work. Here are some options:

1. **Full scope at my standard rate:** [AMOUNT] — everything we discussed, premium quality
2. **Reduced scope at your budget:** [THEIR_BUDGET] — I'd focus on [CORE_DELIVERABLES] and we can phase the rest
3. **Retainer arrangement:** If you have ongoing work, I offer [DISCOUNT]% off for a [DURATION] commitment

Which of these feels right for your situation?

---

## Scenario 2: "We found someone cheaper"

Hi [CLIENT_NAME],

I totally understand — budget matters, and there are certainly options at every price point.

What I'd encourage you to consider is the total cost, not just the rate. In my experience, lower rates often come with:
- More revision rounds (which cost you time)
- Longer timelines (which cost you opportunity)
- Communication gaps (which cost you sanity)

My clients consistently tell me that working with me actually saves them money in the long run because we get it right the first time.

That said, I respect your decision either way. If you'd like to proceed at [YOUR_RATE], I'm ready to start [START_DATE]. If you decide to go another direction, no hard feelings — and my door is always open if you need help down the road.

---

## Scenario 3: "Can you do it for [LOWER_AMOUNT]?"

Hi [CLIENT_NAME],

Thanks for being transparent about the budget. I can't do the full scope at [THEIR_AMOUNT], but here's what I can do:

**At [THEIR_AMOUNT]:** [REDUCED_SCOPE — specific deliverables that fit their budget]
**At [YOUR_RATE]:** [FULL_SCOPE — everything they originally asked for]

The core difference is [WHAT_THEY'D_MISS — e.g., "the responsive design and performance optimization" or "the second round of revisions and the style guide"].

Happy to go either direction — what works best for your goals?

---

## Phrases to Use
- "My rate reflects the value I deliver" (not "I charge X because...")
- "Here are your options" (not "I can't lower my rate")
- "Total cost of the project" (not "my hourly rate")
- "Investment" (not "expense" or "cost")

## Phrases to Avoid
- "I could maybe do it for..." (signals your rate is negotiable)
- "Sorry, my rate is..." (never apologize for your rate)
- "I need to charge X because of my expenses" (your costs aren't their problem)
- "Fine, I'll lower it to..." (you just lost all future pricing leverage with this client)
